A 12-week engagement to repurpose 4 service lines and recover $24M in margin.

Pacific NW asked three firms how to respond to commercial volume migration to ASCs. Here's our answer: keep the orthopedic and cardiac platforms, repurpose two underperforming lines, consolidate ambulatory access.
01 · cover
Our reading of the brief

You're losing the routine; you should be doubling down on the complex.

ASCs took 18% of Pacific NW commercial knee/hip volume in 2024. The natural reaction is competition — open your own ASCs. The better answer: cede the routine, claim the complex. Tertiary referrals are growing, ASCs can't do them, and your tertiary margin is 3.4× ambulatory. We've modeled this for 8 systems; it works.

Where we differ from your other bidders: We won't pitch you ASCs. We'll show you why your facility footprint is your moat, and how to use it.
02 · point of view
What we'd do · 12 weeks

Three workstreams, sequenced.

WS 1

Service line review

4-line review (ortho, cardiac, GI, OB-GYN) — clinical depth, payer mix, margin walk. Output: keep / shrink / exit recommendation.

WS 2

Tertiary growth model

Modeling tertiary case capture from 14 referring facilities. Builds 24-month referral plan and dedicated coordinator function.

WS 3

Ambulatory consolidation

32 ambulatory sites → 18 with hub-and-spoke design. Capacity, drive-time, and physician-effort modeling.

Sequencing: WS 1 outputs feed WS 2 (which lines drive tertiary draw); WS 1+2 feed WS 3 (where to consolidate). All three close week 11; recommendation pack week 12.
