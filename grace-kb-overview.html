SECTION 01
The nine specializations and what good looks like
Gratia delivers across nine specialization clusters. Each has tiered deliverables (familiar = A1/A2 work, proficient = A2/A3, expert = A3/A4) and a quality rubric used during AI QA review. Every deliverable is scored on four universal dimensions plus four specialization-specific dimensions, on a 1 to 5 scale.

Universal dimensions · apply to everything

01

Completeness

All brief requirements addressed.

02

Accuracy

Data, facts, and calculations verified.

03

Presentation

Professional, clear, audience-appropriate.

04

Timeliness

Delivered on schedule with proper communication.

SECTION 04
Promotion criteria
Promotion is a function of three things: AQS, specialization tier scores, and review outcomes on real deliverables.
A1→A2
  • AQS consistently 80+
  • 1+ specialization at Familiar (SS 50+)
  • 3+ deliverables Passed/Exceeded
  • Less supervision needed
A2→A3
  • AQS consistently 90+
  • 1+ Expert (SS 85+)
  • 2+ Proficient or above
  • 5+ deliverables Exceeded
  • 4.5+ avg client rating
A3→A4
  • AQS consistently 95+
  • 2+ Expert (SS 85+)
  • 3+ Proficient or above
  • Reference-quality work
  • Contributes to team standards
A4→L1
  • AQS 95+ sustained
  • 3+ Expert (SS 85+)
  • 4+ Proficient (SS 70+)
  • Demonstrated mentoring impact
L1→M1
  • Manages 5+ analysts
  • Track record developing analysts (A1→A2, A2→A3)
  • VP+ client relationships
  • Operational maturity
Soft restriction on decline. If an analyst's AQS drops below their level threshold for a sustained period, they are not demoted immediately. Instead they keep their title, are flagged internally (not visible to clients), and are not matched to work at their level until AQS recovers. This gives time to recover without the demoralizing impact of a formal demotion.

6.2 AI quality review · the V-flow

first-submission score feeds AQS
  1. 1Analyst submits first draft.
  2. 2AI scores on the 4 universal + 4 spec dimensions. First-submission score is what feeds AQS.
  3. 3AI gives feedback, classifying each point as substantive (must fix) or polish (optional).
  4. 4Analyst revises if needed. Only substantive issues require revision.
  5. 5Client-ready when avg dimension score ≥ 3.5.

6.3–6.5 Other sources

KB quizzes & upskill modules · K-level. Pass mark 80%. K-level evidence does NOT raise the tier score — knowing material isn't the same as applying it. Counts toward engagement signal & AQS Standards.
Portfolio reviews · F-level. Prior work submitted by the analyst is reviewed by the Gratia delivery team and converted into specialization or competency points, up to a 70 cap.
Interview / provisional · P-level. Interview signal at hiring time; gives a low-confidence starting score that gets overwritten as stronger evidence arrives.

9.2 Where Grace sources the data

SOURCE 01

Original client input

Email, intake form, sales call notes — pasted into rawInput. Grace parses with an LLM that maps loose phrasing to the structured service taxonomy ("we need help modeling our Series B" → financial-models + pitch-decks).

SOURCE 02

Organization context

If the client org already exists, Grace enriches parsing with past projects and admin notes to infer likely services and constraints.

SOURCE 03

Iterative re-parse

When the client answers follow-up questions, Grace appends the answers to rawInput and re-parses the whole thing, filling empty fields. Explicit answers always win over inferred values.

Not yet wired up · aspirational

Document upload pipeline

Parsing decks, briefs, contracts attached by client.

Mining of prior similar projects

Pull lessons / scope from past Gratia engagements.

Summary-from-chat synthesis

Auto-generate brief from a sales call transcript.

9.3 How we find gaps

The detectGaps step

examines 12 critical fields

Fields examined: route, areas, description, seniority, hours, duration, timeline, timezone overlap, tools, collaboration model, constraints

  • Empty fields and "unclear" route flag are candidates.
  • Grace generates 2–5 conversational follow-up questions.
  • Each question includes a smart-default suggestion for client convenience.
  • Priority rule: if route is unclear, that's question #1 and staffing-specific questions wait.
What's missing

There's no confidence score, no required-vs-optional logic, and no mandatory-field enforcement yet. Gaps are heuristic, not rule-based.

9.4 How follow-ups are created

↗Outbound · question to client

  1. detectGaps returns the list of questions.
  2. generateFollowUpEmail wraps them in a warm, concise email that weaves the questions in conversationally (rather than a bulleted form).
  3. Admin reviews and sends; the channel (email or Slack) is tracked.

↙Inbound · client response

  1. Client responds; admin pastes the response into answerGaps.
  2. Grace re-parses original + Q&A → fills in empty fields.
  3. Resolved gaps are removed; brief progresses toward ready.

9.5 Once the brief is ready

generateAnalystPitch
Sends a Slack/email pitch to a candidate analyst that combines:
  • Brief context (scope, tools, seniority, hours)
  • Analyst's CV / experience

Delivery channel and success/failure are tracked.
