## PERSONA 1: FJ Labs Partner (Marketplace Specialist)

**Background:** 900+ marketplace investments. Expert in marketplace mechanics, unit economics, network effects, chicken-and-egg dynamics.

---

### WHAT EXCITES ME

**Slide 7 (Traction at scale): The numbers are real, and the metrics are the right ones.**
- $2M revenue, 3x trajectory, 85% repeat rate. These are hard metrics that tell me PMF exists.
- 270+ projects delivered. That's real throughput, not vanity traction.
- 88.2 NPS. For a marketplace, that's exceptional. That tells me both sides love it.
- Quarterly chart shows consistent acceleration (18% → 28% → 42% → 60% → 85% Q1 '26). This is textbook hockey stick.

**Slide 8 (Apollo enterprise deal): This is the single strongest signal you could show me.**
- Exclusive, multi-year, multi-million deal. This isn't a pilot. This is a customer betting their own operations on you.
- "Referenceable customer" — this kills so much FUD. PE firms will copy what works, and Apollo just validated you work at their scale.
- This proves supply quality at enterprise standards. That's the hardest part of a marketplace.

**Slide 6 (Case study): You found the keystone unit in the flywheel.**
- 2 weeks vs. 7 weeks hiring. That's the real job-to-be-done captured. You're not selling "outsourced labor." You're selling speed.
- "First SaaS project ever" on the analyst side, then 3 follow-ons. That shows you're actually training supply, not just shuffling warm bodies.
- This is two-sided growth in one engagement. That's flywheel mechanics.

**Slide 9 (Business model): 30-40% take rate is healthy marketplace margin.**
- At $55-70/hr client pricing, 30-40% Gratia take means analysts keep $33-49/hr. That's a 3-5x improvement over local rates in emerging markets.
- This solves both sides' math: clients save vs. hiring locally, analysts earn 10x their local alternative. Arbitrage is sustainable.
- 60% gross margin current, 70%+ at scale. That trajectory is credible if you're automating vetting/matching with AI.

**Slide 10 (The Flywheel): You've articulated the actual moat.**
- Five layers, each project feeds data back into all four others. This is how you create switching costs.
- "2,000+ projects → each one makes next faster, cheaper, better." That's the unit of competitive advantage. It's not the analysts; it's the compounding data.
- Competitors can't poach your analysts because they have no credentialing history, no coaching data, no matching signal elsewhere. Lock-in is real.

### WHAT CONCERNS ME

**Slide 2 (Demand): The "cost of bad decisions" framing is weak, and the numbers don't support the urgency.**
- "$15M avg. annual cost per company" for bad data decisions. This is an Actian/IBM number that doesn't specify *your* TAM.
- 93% of orgs can't forecast within 5% — but that doesn't mean they'll buy your marketplace. They'll hire analysts, use Excel, or live with it.
- You're conflating "problem exists" with "customers will pay you to solve it." Show me pull, not push. Where's the customer demand story?

**Slide 3 (Supply): The arbitrage framing may repel enterprise buyers.**
- "Millions of analysts, 10x wage arbitrage." This is *your* story about supply. But it's also a signal to enterprises: "You're paying for geopolitical arbitrage."
- Enterprise buyers worry about this cutting the other way: "What if my analysts see how much the same work pays in emerging markets and demand 10x raises?"
- Reframe supply as "global talent pool unblocked by geography," not "wage arbitrage is the moat."

**Slide 4 (Our Solution): "We create analysts" is marketing fluff without specifics.**
- What does "AI trains the supply" actually mean? Slides 11-14 (the demo) are placeholders ("Scoping UI screenshot"). I have *no idea* what the AI is actually doing.
- How does GraceAI improve outcomes vs. a good manager? Show me before/after quality metrics, not just "AI coaching."
- You claim the flywheel compounds, but I see no evidence that Week 1 delivery improves Week 5 matching. Show the data.

**Slide 5 (Why Now): "Incumbents will build in-house AI training within 2–3 years" is a self-defeating narrative.**
- If true, you've just told me you have 2-3 years to own the category before your customer base learns to do this internally.
- Your response should be: "Incumbents *tried* and failed, here's why" or "Our defensibility means 3 years is more than enough." Instead, you're admitting the moat is temporary.
- This slides into a "race against AI" story instead of a "we're the AI" story. Pick one.

**Slide 9 (Business Model): The unit economics math doesn't add up under scrutiny.**
- $4.5k average SMB engagement. If your client pays $55-70/hr and you take 30-40%, your per-project revenue is roughly $15k-20k (assuming 50-80 hour engagements).
- But $4.5k is much lower. Are SMBs smaller? Or is this blended with the enterprise cohort? This is confusing and raises questions.

**Slide 6 & 8: Where is the churn story?**
- 85% repeat rate is great, but what about the other 15%? More importantly: *how many projects does the average client do?*
- If a client does 2-3 projects and churns, 85% repeat is less impressive than if they do 10+ across a year.

**Slide 15 (Positioning): You claim "no one else is both fast AND trusted," but that's not true.**
- Consulting firms are trusted. They're slow, but Bain/McKinsey have zero competition on trust.
- Freelance platforms are fast but unvetted. You're in the middle.
- Your positioning should be: "Fast + trusted + globally-distributed supply at affordable prices." That's different and defensible. Current framing is incomplete.

**Slide 18 (Team): Impressive resume pedigree, but where's the marketplace operator?**
- Jackie (CEO) built the marketplace from zero. Good.
- Norbert (CTO) came from Kasa. Good product-scaling credentials.
- But where's your CFO or Head of Operations who has scaled a two-sided marketplace specifically?

**Slide 7: No breakdown of project mix — are you really creating new supply or just trafficking existing analysts?**
- 270+ projects from 150+ analysts. That's 1.8 projects per analyst on average.
- Are these the same analysts or new ones each time? If the same, you're not "creating supply"; you're optimizing allocation of existing capacity.
- This is a critical distinction for marketplace defensibility. I need to see analyst churn, new supply sourcing, and retention data.

**Cautious yes, with conditions.**

**Why:**
- Real traction ($2M ARR, 85% repeat, 88.2 NPS) beats most pitches I see. You've found PMF in both supply and demand.
- Apollo deal is a massive differentiator. Enterprise validation at scale is rare and valuable.
- The problem is real. Hiring cycles are slow, talent is distributed, and arbitrage exists.
- Your flywheel narrative is strong if the data backs it up.

**But with serious reservations:**
- You need to prove the flywheel actually compounds. One case study doesn't cut it.
- The unit economics story is fuzzy. Show me the full LTV/CAC/payback analysis.
- Enterprise sales motion is unknown. One deal could be a customer success miracle, not a playbook.
- The "2-3 year urgency" narrative undermines your own defensibility thesis. You're signaling your moat has an expiration date.
- The "adjacent vertical" vision is speculative and depends on data portability that may not exist.

**Second meeting agenda:**
1. Walk me through flywheel data (matching quality improvement over time).
2. Enterprise sales playbook and win rate.
3. Full unit economics model by cohort and segment.
4. Competitive scenario planning: What's your response if Upwork or BCG clones you?
5. Deep dive on analyst retention, utilization, and local market dynamics.

**My read:** You have a real business with real momentum. But you're asking me to believe in a category that may not exist yet. I need to see more proof that marketplace economics compound, that enterprise customers stick, and that your supply isn't just commoditized freelancers with better vetting. If those three things are true, this is a strong Series A. If not, you're a nice SaaS business that might plateau at $10M ARR.
