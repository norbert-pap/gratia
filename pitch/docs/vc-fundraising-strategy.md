## 2.3: EMAIL TEMPLATE BEATS (Not Full Email — Use as Framework)

**Beat 1: Opening Hook** (15-20 words)

- OR: Name a portfolio company + how Gratia relates
- Goal: Show you've done research

- Point they already know intellectually but haven't acted on
- Specific to their portfolio (e.g., "We're seeing PE firms struggle to hire senior analysts quickly")

**Beat 3: Gratia Solution** (20-30 words)
- 1 sentence, max
- AVOID: Lengthy feature description
- DO: Frame as "this is what the market needs"

Example: "Gratia is the on-demand marketplace solving that via AI-matched analysts."

- Metrics (revenue, repeat rate, NPS)

- Goal: Differentiate you from 100 cold emails they get

**Beat 5: Clear Ask** (10-15 words)
- "20 min call next week?"
- NOT: "Let me know if you want to chat"
- Goal: Remove friction; specific date/time optional

**Beat 6: Personalized Close** (5-10 words)
- Something unique to them or research done
- Not a generic sign-off

## 2.6: POSITIONING THE ROUND — "First Institutional, Not Seed"

### Key Message: This Is a Series A, Not a Seed

**Why This Matters:**
- Seed = uncertain PMF, high risk, smaller checks ($1-5M)
- Series A = proven PMF, lower risk, larger checks ($10M+), faster deployment

**Messaging:**
- "We've built our business with angels + bootstrapped revenue; Series A is natural next step to accelerate team + product"
- NOT: "We're finally raising VC" (sounds uncertain)
- DO: "We've already PMF-validated; Series A lets us scale faster"

**Slide Language:**
- Headline: "Series A to Accelerate Proven Marketplace" (not "Seed Round")
- Note: All growth from revenue + angels (shows capital efficiency)

**Investor Psychology:**
- VCs want to feel like they're funding acceleration of proven product, not validating hypothesis
- Your traction (revenue, repeat rate, NPS, Apollo) proves you've already validated
- This justifies higher valuation + larger check

## 3.2: IDEAL PARALLEL CONVERSATION TRACKS

### Track A: Lead Investor (Primary)
- Start with 1-2 Tier 1 VCs most excited
- Goal: Get lead term sheet by Week 8
- Parallel meetings with 2-3 other leads (don't let one VC block you)

### Track B: Co-Investor Participants (Secondary)
- Start with Tier 1 VCs + FJ Labs once lead is "likely"
- Frame: "Lead investor is [big name]; we have room for 2-3 co-investors"
- Goal: Secure 60% of round from lead + 1-2 co-investors by Week 12

### Track C: Strategic Investors (Tertiary, Optional)
- Approach Tier 2 slower-moving VCs in Week 6-8 (after lead is clear)
- Frame: "We're progressing with lead; are you interested in small allocation?"
- Goal: Fill remaining 20% of round; adds diversification

**Key Rule:** Don't pursue 25 VCs equally; focus fire on 5-7 leads, then expand.

## 3.4: CREATING FOMO / MOMENTUM

### Week 2-3: Establish Investor Scarcity Mindset
**Action:** In first meetings, say:
- "We're moving quickly—probably 10-12 week timeline to close"
- "We're talking to leading investors across [marketplace, AI, future-of-work]"
- "If you're interested, now's the time to stay close"

**Why:** Sets expectation that you're prioritizing interested leads; laggards will feel pressure.

---

### Week 4-5: Mention 2-3 Interested Leads (Without Breaking Confidentiality)
**Action:** In follow-ups, drop hints:
- "a16z and Craft are both excited; we're in full pitch conversations with each"
- "We're likely to have a lead investor decided by Week 7-8"
- "Would you want to participate or lead?"

**Why:** FOMO drives action. VCs don't want to miss momentum.

---

### Week 6-7: Explicit Signal of Lead Intention
**Action:** When 1 VC is clearly leading, tell other VCs:
- Email: "[VC Name] is our lead investor; we have $[X]M room for [2-3] co-investors. Interested?"
- Verbal in calls: "We're excited about [Lead] as lead; they bring [strategic value]. We want co-investors who add specific value."

**Why:** Activates decision-making. VCs prefer to co-invest with known leads than make solo bets.

---

### Week 8-10: Closing Mentality
**Action:**
- Schedule co-investor calls with tighter timeline ("We're closing by Week X")
- Share term sheet structure (signals seriousness, creates deadline)
- Use: "Last allocation spot available"

---

## 3.5: MANAGING MULTIPLE VCS IN PARALLEL (Don't Let One Block You)

### Best Practice: 3-5 Parallel Leads (Not Just 1)

**Week 1-3:**
- Build relationships with 3-5 Tier 1 VCs simultaneously
- Don't tell any one they're your "lead" yet
- Let them compete for the position

**Week 4-5:**
- Full pitches to all 3-5
- Note which one moves fastest / asks smartest questions / brings most strategic value

**Week 6-8:**
- Parallel IC meetings with 2-3 most interested
- Let them know about other ICs (creates urgency)
- Negotiate term sheets with 2-3 simultaneously (legal complexity but worth it)

**Week 8-10:**
- Decide on lead based on: term sheet terms, strategic value, speed, partner relationship
- Pick the strongest + most aligned

**Why This Works:**
- Avoids getting stuck waiting for slow VC (happens often)
- Gives you leverage in term sheet negotiation
- Multiple leads competing = better terms for you

# PART 4: INVESTOR SELECTION FRAMEWORK (After You Have Options)

## 4.1: HOW TO CHOOSE BETWEEN MULTIPLE TERM SHEETS

If you're lucky enough to have multiple term sheets, evaluate on:

### 1. **Terms** (30% weight)
- Valuation (reasonable for traction + stage)
- Liquidation preference (non-participating preferred stock is founder-friendly)
- Board seat (do you want them in governance?)
- Pro-rata rights (can they follow in Series B?)

### 2. **Strategic Value** (40% weight)
- Do they have enterprise relationships? (Important for Gratia's enterprise sales)
- Marketplace expertise? (Craft > others)
- AI portfolio? (Menlo, Lightspeed, General Catalyst bring AI ops knowledge)
- Apollo ecosystem? (Which VC is most connected to Apollo / PE firms?)

**For Gratia specifically:**
- Prioritize VCs who understand marketplace unit economics
- Prioritize VCs who can open PE firm doors (Series B customers)
- Prioritize VCs with B2B SaaS sales expertise (Gratia's go-to-market)

### 3. **Partner Relationship** (30% weight)
- Do you want to spend 4+ years with this person?
- Do they listen + ask great questions?
- Do they respond to emails?
- Are they helpful in non-monetary ways (intros, advice)?

**Red flags:**
- VC who doesn't listen in meetings
- VC who takes 3+ weeks to respond to emails
- VC who only talks about other portfolio companies
