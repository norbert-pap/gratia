# Gratia Pitch Deck — VC Alignment & Metrics Recommendations

*Prepared Feb 2026 for Norbert Pap / Gratia seed raise*

---

## Executive Summary

Your deck tells a strong story, but it's currently structured as a **product narrative** rather than a **VC investment narrative**. The biggest gaps are: (1) placeholder metrics that leave investors guessing, (2) missing the marketplace-specific metrics VCs use to evaluate stage-readiness, and (3) not fully leveraging your triple-thesis positioning (marketplace + AI + future of work). Below is a slide-by-slide breakdown plus metric benchmarks calibrated to seed-stage marketplace standards.

---

## Part 1: Narrative Framing — What to Fix

### Your Deck's Current Thesis: "We solve an analyst shortage with AI-trained talent"

This is a solid **problem thesis**, but VCs don't fund problems — they fund **business models with compounding advantages**. Your deck needs to answer the three questions every generalist seed investor asks:

1. **Why is this a $500M+ business?** (You address this, but the TAM slide needs sharper bottoms-up logic)
2. **Why does this get better with scale?** (Your flywheel is buried in the product section — it should be a headline)
3. **Why now, and why can't this be copied?** (The "window closes in 2–3 years" framing on slide 3 is excellent — but your moats slide undersells the AI data moat)

### Recommended Narrative Arc

Reframe the deck around this progression:

> **Problem** → **Why now** (timing window) → **Solution** (marketplace + AI flywheel) → **Traction proving the flywheel works** → **Unit economics that improve with scale** → **Enterprise validation as proof of quality** → **Market size** → **Team** → **The ask**

Key changes:

- **Move "Why Now" higher.** Your supply-side slide (slide 3) makes the strongest timing argument in the deck. Consider combining slides 2 and 3 into a single, tighter problem statement and giving the "window closing" argument its own beat.
- **Lead with the flywheel, not the product.** Slide 8 (Product) describes a workflow. What VCs want to see is a **compounding loop**: projects → data → better AI → better matching → more projects. Frame the product as the flywheel engine, not a feature list.
- **Put unit economics before market size.** Investors need to believe the model works at the micro level before they care about the macro opportunity. Your business model slide (14) should come right after traction, not after positioning.

## Part 3: Benchmark Recommendations for Your Placeholders

Based on your business model (managed talent marketplace, $18–$210/hr tiers, AI-augmented delivery), here's what I'd recommend for each placeholder:

### Take Rate: Target 30–40%

Talent marketplaces operate at higher take rates than product marketplaces because you're providing vetting, matching, QA, and AI tooling — not just connecting two parties. Comparable benchmarks: Toptal takes ~30%, staffing agencies take 30–50%, and Catalant (strategy talent marketplace) operates at ~35%. Your AI coaching layer justifies the higher end. **Show this as a range (e.g., "30–40% blended take rate") rather than a single number** — it signals tiered pricing sophistication.

### Gross Margin: Target 55–65% (on Net Revenue)

**Important accounting note:** Gross margin for marketplaces is calculated on *net revenue* (your take), not on GMV. Analyst payouts are pass-through costs that never hit your P&L. Your COGS on net revenue includes: AI infrastructure, QA/delivery overhead, project management labor, and platform costs.

Example: On $100 GMV at 35% take rate → $35 net revenue → ~$13 in COGS (AI, QA, ops) → ~$22 gross profit → **63% gross margin.**

At 30–40% take rate with lean operations, 55–65% gross margin on net revenue is achievable and credible. **VCs will want to see a path to 70%+ as AI automation reduces delivery overhead.** Show current margin AND projected margin at scale. Be explicit about your accounting basis — VCs get confused when marketplace founders conflate GMV-based and net-revenue-based margins.

### Average Engagement Value: Target $4K–$6K

Based on your $55–70/hr most popular tier and typical analytical projects running 60–100 hours, a $4K–$6K average engagement is realistic. If your Apollo deal skews this higher, break it out: "$4.5K avg. SMB engagement, $15K+ avg. enterprise engagement."

### YoY Revenue Growth: Target 200–300%+

At seed stage with 270+ projects delivered and quarterly growth visible in your bar chart, you should be able to show 3–4x year-over-year growth. The FJ Labs matrix expects seed-stage marketplaces to demonstrate ~300% annual GMV growth to be Series A-ready in 18 months.

### Valuation: $15M–$22M Pre-Money

The median seed pre-money for AI startups in 2025 is ~$17.9M. Given your: (a) real revenue and 270+ delivered projects, (b) Apollo enterprise deal, (c) AI positioning, and (d) marketplace model with network effects — you can justify the upper range. A $5M raise at $18–22M pre-money (20–25% dilution) is defensible if you frame traction well.

### Prior Raised: Show It

Whatever you've raised to date, show it. If you've been capital-efficient (built this far on <$1M), that's a massive signal. VCs love seeing revenue-per-dollar-of-capital-raised. If you bootstrapped to current traction, say that prominently.

## Part 4: Slide-by-Slide Recommendations

### Slide 1 (Cover)
- **"The on-demand business analyst marketplace"** is descriptive but flat. Consider a tagline that captures the flywheel: *"AI-trained analysts. On-demand. Decision-grade."* or *"We create the analysts the market needs — and match them to the work."*

### Slide 2 (Problem / Demand)
- Strong content. Consider adding a specific dollar figure for the cost of delayed decisions: "Every week without analyst capacity costs mid-market companies $XX in delayed revenue."
- The Deloitte and Workiva citations are good. Make sure these are checkable — VCs will verify.

### Slide 3 (Problem / Supply)
- This is your best slide for "why now" — the convergence of AI, remote work, and eroding career paths. Consider renaming this section from "Problem / Supply" to **"Why Now"** — it's more compelling and maps to how VCs think.
- The "window closes in 2–3 years" claim is powerful. Back it up: what happens after the window? (e.g., "Incumbents build in-house AI training, large platforms move upmarket.")

### Slide 4 (Solution)
- The demand ↔ Gratia ↔ supply diagram is good architecture but needs more specificity. Add the flywheel arrows: "Every project trains the AI, credentials the analyst, and improves matching." You say this on slide 8 — pull it forward.

### Slide 5 (Case Study)
- Good dual-sided story. Consider adding hard numbers: "Delivered a board-ready unit economics model in 14 days for $X. Client's alternative: $Y from a consulting firm or 7 weeks to hire."

### Slide 6 (Traction at Scale)
**This is the most important slide in your deck and it needs the most work.**

Current metrics shown: 270+ projects, 150+ analysts, 85% repeat rate, 88.2 NPS. These are strong engagement metrics but they're **activity metrics, not business metrics**. VCs will immediately ask: "What's the revenue?"

Add to this slide:
- Monthly or annual GMV
- Monthly or annual net revenue
- MoM or QoQ revenue growth rate
- Average engagement value
- One or two unit economics numbers (gross margin, LTV:CAC if available)

The growth chart (quarterly projects delivered) should ideally be a **revenue chart** instead, or shown alongside a revenue chart.

### Slide 7 (Enterprise Validation — Apollo)
- Very strong. "Multi-million dollar deal" is good but vague. If you can share an actual number range or annual contract value, do it. If not, at minimum say "multi-year partnership" to signal durability.
- Add: "Referenceable customer" if Apollo is willing to speak with investors. This is gold at seed stage.

### Slide 8 (Product)
- Rename from "Product" to **"The Flywheel"** or **"How It Compounds."** VCs care less about product features and more about why the product creates a moat.
- The "Every project trains the AI and credentials the analyst — data moat compounds" line at the bottom is your most important sentence. Move it to the top.

### Slides 9–12 (Demo)
- Four demo slides is a lot for a fundraise deck. Consider collapsing to one slide with a link to a 90-second video. In live pitch, you can talk through the demo verbally while showing one screenshot.

### Slide 13 (Positioning & Moats)
- The 2x2 positioning map is strong. One issue: you have four moats listed (network effects, proprietary data, enterprise lock-in, trust infrastructure) — for a seed-stage company, this feels aspirational rather than current. **Separate current moats from moats-in-progress.** E.g., "Current: 150+ analyst head start, proprietary training data from 270+ projects. Building: Enterprise lock-in via Apollo, trust infrastructure."

### Slide 14 (Business Model)
- This is where all your placeholder metrics live. Filling them in per the benchmarks above is critical.
- **Add a "path to improving margins" narrative.** Show current take rate and gross margin, then show where AI automation takes them. E.g., "Current GM: 58%. With full AI automation: 72%. Every project moves us closer."
- The tier pricing ladder ($18–$210/hr) is good but consider showing **where your current revenue concentrates**. If 70% of revenue is at the $55–70 tier, say that — it helps VCs model your average revenue per hour.

### Slide 15 (Market Opportunity)
- TAM/SAM/SOM structure is fine but the assumptions are all placeholders. **Bottom-up TAM is more credible than top-down at seed stage.** Try: "X analysts working Y hours/year at $Z/hour = $SOM. We need N% market share to reach $[Series A target]."
- $85B TAM → $12B SAM → $500M SOM. The SAM-to-SOM ratio (24:1) is fine. The TAM-to-SAM ratio (7:1) should be justified with clear scoping criteria.

### Slide 16 (Team)
- Good team slide. Consider adding: (a) how you know each other / why this team, (b) any advisors or angels that add credibility, especially marketplace or PE/VC operators.

### Slide 17 (The Ask)
- $5M seed, 50/30/20 allocation to product/supply/sales — this is clear and well-structured.
- The 12-month milestones (500+ analysts, $3M ARR, 5 enterprise partners, self-serve launch) are ambitious but specific. **Make sure $3M ARR is consistent with your current run rate + growth expectations.** If you're at ~$500K ARR now, $3M in 12 months implies ~6x growth — aggressive but not impossible with enterprise deals.
- **Add a "what this unlocks" line:** "$3M ARR and 5 enterprise partners positions us for a $15–20M Series A in Q1 2027."

## Part 5: Metrics You Should NOT Show

A few things to avoid that seed-stage marketplace founders sometimes include but that backfire:

- **Projected revenue 3+ years out.** At seed stage, 3-year projections have zero credibility. Show 12–18 month milestones max.
- **Total registered analysts** if most aren't billing. Show "vetted and active" not "signed up." Your 150+ seems vetted, which is good — make that explicit.
- **Cumulative GMV** without showing monthly trends. Cumulative always goes up. MoM growth is what matters.
- **Blended NPS across both sides** if one side drags it down. If both sides are 80+, great — show them separately for extra impact.
