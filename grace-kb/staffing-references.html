Staffing references · domain knowledge for matching

Reference material Grace can read when matching analysts to specialty client requests. Different from Section 1 (deliverables and QA rubrics): these entries describe who can credibly do this work, what to look for in a CV, and what to never confuse it with.

Status · v0 draft. Content lives in the website repo at app/.server/ai/prompts/staffing-references/ and is not yet wired into Grace's runtime context. The README in that folder documents the entry format and how the loading logic will work once the format is signed off.
11.0
Why this exists

Without grounding material, Grace pattern-matches on broad terms ("finance," "investment") instead of specific sub-disciplines ("private credit," "State Street PAM"). Result: junior IB bankers recommended for private-credit roles, supply-chain "PAM" CV hits surfaced for State Street system requests. These references give Grace the disambiguators a finance professional carries in their head.

Every entry — whether a service category or an industry — uses the same six fields: Definition, Not to be confused with, CV signals (positive), CV signals (false positive), Adjacent / transferable from, and Levels. Where a domain practitioner is needed to validate, a [NEEDS_EXPERT_REVIEW] marker is left in place — concrete asks for Jackie / domain experts instead of a vague "send us material."

11.1.1 Financial modeling & valuation Forecasting · scenarios · valuation

Cluster scope. Decision-ready financial models and planning frameworks that translate intent into defensible forecasts, quantified risk scenarios, and valuation-backed capital decisions. Three categories below — staff to the right one.

Forecasting & Planning

Definition. Building integrated financial models (3-statement, driver-based, or operating models) that connect revenue drivers, cost structures, and cash flow dynamics — for planning, fundraising, or board decisions.

Not to be confused with. Budgeting / FP&A reporting (variance work, not model construction); scenario & sensitivity analysis (testing existing models); dashboard / KPI reporting (visualization on top, not the underlying model).

CV signals · positive. 3-statement build experience; driver-based forecasting; advanced Excel (INDIRECT, OFFSET, INDEX/MATCH, dynamic arrays); Anaplan / Adaptive / Cube / Pigment / Vena; FP&A or strategic finance roles; named deliverables ("Series B model," "5-year forecast," "operating plan").

CV signals · false positive. "Financial modeling" listed without a deliverable (often classroom exposure); "FP&A" alone (could be reporting only); junior IB analysts who built valuation models, not operating ones.

Adjacent / transferable from. Junior IB / MBB consulting (if they owned a build); corporate strategy with operating-plan exposure; founders who built their own model.

Levels. A2 supports; A3+ owns; A4 / M1 leads complex builds.

Scenario & Sensitivity Analysis

Definition. Structured scenario planning that quantifies upside, downside, and break-even outcomes on top of an existing model, to support decisions under uncertainty.

Not to be confused with. Forecasting & planning (the underlying model itself); operational risk management (different domain); Monte Carlo / quantitative simulation (a higher bar — flag if the request implies it).

CV signals · positive. Built sensitivity tables / data tables in Excel; scenario manager or named scenarios; tornado charts; break-even analysis; scenarios on revenue / margin / churn / unit economics with explicit assumption shifts.

CV signals · false positive. "Scenario planning" in a strategy-consulting context sometimes means qualitative futures planning, not financial sensitivity.

Adjacent / transferable from. Corporate development (M&A model sensitivities); equity research (bear / base / bull case); VC / PE associates (LBO sensitivities, multiple-on-money cases).

Levels. A3+ owns; A2 with supervision.

Company & Asset Valuation

Definition. Defensible valuation analysis using DCF, comparable companies / precedent transactions, LBO, or asset-based methods — for transactions, fundraising, or internal planning.

Not to be confused with. Real estate appraisal (different methodology); insurance / actuarial valuation (reserving and pricing, not corporate); forecasting & planning (forecast is an input to a DCF, but operating planning is a separate skill).

CV signals · positive. DCF / WACC build / terminal value; comparable companies; precedent transactions; LBO; IB analyst (M&A or coverage); PE associate; VC investor; corp dev; CFA charter or progress — weight against deliverable evidence. [NEEDS_EXPERT_REVIEW: how heavily to weight CFA progress vs. real deliverables]

CV signals · false positive. "Valuation" listed without a deliverable (often a touched comps page, not a built valuation); equity research juniors with public-company exposure but limited private-company nuance.

Adjacent / transferable from. Equity research (rigor transfers, private-company nuance may not); restructuring / TAS at Big 4; founders who raised institutional capital.

Levels. A3+ owns; A4 / M1 for complex / multi-method valuations.

11.1.2 Market research & intelligence Sizing · competitive · customer research

Cluster scope. Actionable market research and competitive intelligence to pressure-test strategy, de-risk new markets, and give leaders confidence in every move. "Market research" is one of the most overloaded terms on a CV — before staffing, force the requester to specify which of the three categories below applies. The skill profiles diverge meaningfully.

Market Sizing & Opportunity Assessment

Definition. Quantifying addressable markets (TAM / SAM / SOM), validating segment attractiveness, and prioritizing opportunities for entry, expansion, or investment. Output is a defensible number built from primary inputs and triangulated — not a desk-research summary.

Not to be confused with. Competitive analysis (sister category — that's about rivals); customer research (the buyer view, not the aggregate); forecasting & planning (a company's revenue forecast, not the market upper bound); industry overview / desk research (analyst-report copy-paste, not a built number).

CV signals · positive. TAM / SAM / SOM with shown methodology (top-down vs. bottom-up, ideally both with triangulation); MBB (BCG, Bain, McKinsey) on commercial DD or growth strategy; tier-2 strategy (L.E.K., Roland Berger, OC&C); industry analyst firms (Gartner, Forrester, IDC, Frost & Sullivan, Euromonitor, Mintel); tools: Statista, IBISWorld, Crunchbase, PitchBook, CB Insights, Capital IQ; methodology references: Fermi estimation, comparable-market analogy, bottoms-up unit-economics build.

CV signals · false positive. "Market research" as a one-line skill (often means "I read industry reports"); sell-side equity research (strong on listed-market sizing, less reps on private/emerging markets); IB pitchbook prep (single comps page from a broker report).

Adjacent / transferable from. Commercial due diligence at consulting (closest cousin); equity research / VC associates (industry sizing chops, confirm private-market exposure); product managers who built TAM decks for fundraising; industry analysts (strong methodology, sometimes light on commercial framing).

Levels. A2 supports under guidance; A3+ owns a build; A4 / M1 leads cross-segment or multi-geo sizing.

Competitive Analysis & Benchmarking

Definition. Systematic study of competitors — their offerings, positioning, pricing, GTM, performance, and strategy — to inform a client's competitive moves. Output: a defensible "where we stand and what to do about it" picture, not a list of rivals.

Not to be confused with. Market sizing (the size of the prize, not who's competing); customer research (the buyer view); industry overview (broader, less actionable); IB competitor pages (often pitchbook fodder copy-pasted from broker reports).

CV signals · positive. Frameworks named: Porter's Five Forces, strategic group mapping, value-curve / Blue Ocean canvas, Wardley mapping; concepts: battlecards, win / loss analysis, deal-loss program, competitor teardowns, feature parity matrix, pricing benchmark; roles: competitive intel at B2B SaaS, corporate strategy, MBB commercial DD, Klue / Crayon (CI platforms), product marketing; industry analyst work at Gartner (Magic Quadrant) or Forrester (Wave).

CV signals · false positive. "Competitor research" used loosely (could be "I checked their LinkedIn"); marketing roles that did "competitive monitoring" (surveillance is not analysis).

Adjacent / transferable from. Product marketing managers (positioning, battlecards); strategy consulting commercial DD analysts (closest cousin); sales enablement (battlecards, lighter on synthesis); industry analysts at Gartner / Forrester (strong methodology).

Levels. A2 supports; A3+ owns a competitive teardown; A4 / M1 for executive-facing playbooks.

Customer Research & Segmentation

Definition. Understanding buyer needs, pain points, decision drivers, and behaviors to inform product, marketing, or strategy decisions. Both qualitative (interviews, focus groups, ethnography) and quantitative (surveys, conjoint, max-diff, Van Westendorp) methods. Segmentation: clustering customers into actionable groups.

Not to be confused with. Market sizing (how big, not who); competitive analysis (rivals, not buyers); customer success or service ops (managing post-sale, not researching); A/B testing or product analytics (behavioral data on existing users, narrower scope); UX research (usability-focused on a product, not commercial / segmentation).

CV signals · positive. Methodologies: Jobs-to-be-Done (JTBD), customer interviews with documented protocol, conjoint analysis, max-diff, Van Westendorp pricing studies, Kano model, NPS programs, persona development; tools: Qualtrics, SurveyMonkey, UserTesting, Dovetail, Sawtooth (conjoint); roles: market research analyst (corporate or agency), consumer insights at FMCG / CPG, customer / commercial strategy at MBB, voice-of-customer programs.

CV signals · false positive. "Customer research" without methodology (could mean "interviewed 3 people" — look for sample sizes and methods); "user research" in a product context (real research, but usability-focused); marketing analysts who only ran reports on existing data vs. those who designed and conducted primary research.

Adjacent / transferable from. UX researchers (strong qualitative, scaffolding on commercial framing); marketing analytics (strong quantitative, less primary-research design); consumer insights at FMCG / CPG (deep methodology training); strategy consultants on customer / growth projects.

Levels. A2 supports (interview synthesis, survey support); A3+ owns research design and analysis; A4 / M1 leads end-to-end primary research programs.

11.2.1 Private credit Direct lending · mezzanine · distressed

Definition. Non-bank lending to companies, typically by investment funds. Sub-segments: direct lending (senior secured loans to mid-market), mezzanine, distressed / special situations, venture debt. Borrowers are often PE-owned or pre-IPO. The fund holds the loan rather than syndicating it through a public market.

Not to be confused with. M&A advisory / general IB (advises on equity transactions, not lending — the most common false positive); public credit / fixed income trading (markets-driven on listed paper); commercial banking (different regulatory framework, different risk tolerance); actuarial / insurance underwriting (same word, different domain).

CV signals · positive. Firms: Apollo, Ares, Golub, Blackstone Credit (formerly GSO), KKR Credit, Owl Rock / Blue Owl, Sixth Street, Oaktree, Antares, Monroe, Audax, BlackRock Private Credit, Fortress. Functions: origination, credit underwriting, portfolio monitoring, restructuring / workouts. Concepts in CV: unitranche, first lien / second lien, PIK toggle, revolver, DDTL, ABL, covenant-lite, EBITDA add-backs, OID. Loan servicing / fund admin for direct lending vehicles (operations angle).

CV signals · false positive. "Worked on a credit deal at Goldman" — was the candidate selling to credit funds (advisory) or buying for a credit fund (investing)? Different jobs. M&A bankers at firms with credit affiliates sometimes claim "credit experience" without underwriting reps.

Adjacent / transferable from. Leveraged finance / LevFin at IB (same documents, transactional vs. holding mindset); restructuring / TAS at Big 4 (strong on distressed, lighter on origination); corporate or middle-market banking (close, different risk tolerance and structures); PE deal-team analysts (debt understood from the equity-sponsor seat).

Common ask wedge. "Private credit experience" is usually one of three things — clarify before searching: (1) credit underwriting rigor, (2) document fluency (credit agreements, term sheets, amendments), (3) operations / middle-office for a credit fund. The ideal candidate differs significantly across the three. [NEEDS_EXPERT_REVIEW: validate firm list, sub-segment definitions, and the three-way ask-wedge framing]

Levels. A3+ for credit analysis support; A4 / M1 for memo or covenant ownership; lead-level for client-facing engagement on origination or portfolio work.

11.2.2 Insurance · regulatory & operations NAIC · stat accounting · Solvency II

Definition. The insurance sector, with emphasis on regulatory compliance, statutory accounting and reporting (NAIC in the US), and operations supporting insurance companies. Sub-segments: P&C, Life, Health, Reinsurance. Scoped here to the regulatory / operations / advisory angle — actuarial pricing and reserving is a separate specialism.

Not to be confused with. Actuarial science / pricing / reserving (mortality and claims modeling — usually requires FSA / FCAS / ACAS); insurance brokerage / sales (selling policies); insurtech (software for the industry); healthcare consulting (different domain — provider / payer ops); bank regulatory consulting (similar muscle, different rulebook).

CV signals · positive. Firms: AIG, MetLife, Prudential, Allianz, Zurich, Munich Re, Swiss Re, Hartford, Travelers, Chubb, Aon, Marsh, WTW, Big 4 insurance practices. Concepts: NAIC reporting, stat accounting (STAT vs. GAAP), Schedule D, RBC, ORSA, Solvency II, IFRS 17, ALM. Deliverables: annual / quarterly stat filings, RBC calculations, loss ratio analysis, stat-to-GAAP reconciliation.

CV signals · false positive. "Insurance" in the CV because the candidate had an insurance client at a strategy consulting firm but did generic strategy work. "Risk management" at non-insurance firms (different risk).

Adjacent / transferable from. Big 4 audit (insurance practice); actuarial analysts without full credentials; bank regulatory consulting (Basel / CCAR); corporate accounting at an insurer.

Common ask wedge. Three buckets — ask which: (1) regulatory project support (NAIC / state filings); (2) investment accounting for insurers (where State Street PAM-type system experience overlaps); (3) operational transformation (policy admin, claims, billing). The Venn of analysts who can credibly do all three is small. [NEEDS_EXPERT_REVIEW: insurance practitioner to validate concept list, ask-wedge, and adjacent-skill weighting]

Levels. A3+ for stat reporting support; A4 / M1 for ownership of regulatory project workstreams.

11.2.3 Investment banking · M&A advisory Sell-side / buy-side · IPOs · valuation

Definition. Sell-side and buy-side advisory on equity transactions: M&A, IPOs, strategic alternatives, fairness opinions, capital markets transactions. The bank advises and supports execution; it does not take principal risk on the transaction. Included here both as a real client request and as the canonical disambiguator for private-credit and other finance asks.

Not to be confused with. Private credit / direct lending (see 11.2.1 — the most common false positive); equity research (same firms, no transactions); sales & trading (markets, not advisory); corporate development (in-house M&A, one client only); private equity (buys companies, uses IB); venture capital (early-stage investing).

CV signals · positive. Bulge bracket: GS, MS, JPM, BofA, Citi, Barclays, CS / UBS, DB. Elite boutiques: Evercore, Lazard, Centerview, Moelis, HL, Jefferies, PJT, Guggenheim. Middle market: Harris Williams, William Blair, Lincoln, Piper Sandler, Stifel, Raymond James. Concepts: pitched / ran diligence / managed data room / drafted CIM / executed deal / negotiated SPA / fairness opinion. Deliverables: pitch decks, IM, valuation models (DCF / comps / precedents / LBO), management presentations, board materials.

CV signals · false positive. "Investment banking" used loosely (could mean any function at a bank); SA / internship-only experience (pitchbook prep, not execution); "worked on M&A" without a named transaction.

Adjacent / transferable from. Big 4 transaction advisory (strong on diligence, lighter on execution); corp dev (buy-side perspective, fewer reps); PE associates (combine IB execution and investing); MBB on commercial-due-diligence projects (adjacent to IB diligence).

Common pitfall. Recommending an IB analyst for a private-credit role is the canonical false positive. Same modeling rigor, fundamentally different job: IB is transactional / deal-by-deal; private credit is portfolio / hold-and-monitor. Likewise, IB analysts asked to do operating-plan / forecasting work often struggle — they have built valuation models, not operating ones. Confirm what the client actually needs.

Levels. A3+ for support on IB-flavored work; A4 / M1 for ownership; lead-level for client-facing on deal-flavored projects.
