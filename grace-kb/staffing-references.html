Staffing references · domain knowledge for matching

Reference material Grace can read when matching analysts to specialty client requests. Different from Section 1 (deliverables and QA rubrics): these entries describe who can credibly do this work, what to look for in a CV, and what to never confuse it with.

11.0
Why this exists

Without grounding material, Grace pattern-matches on broad terms ("finance," "investment") instead of specific sub-disciplines ("private credit," "State Street PAM"). Result: junior IB bankers recommended for private-credit roles, supply-chain "PAM" CV hits surfaced for State Street system requests. These references give Grace the disambiguators a finance professional carries in their head.

Every entry — whether a service category or an industry — uses the same six fields: Definition, Not to be confused with, CV signals (positive), CV signals (false positive), Adjacent / transferable from, and Levels. Where a domain practitioner is needed to validate, a [NEEDS_EXPERT_REVIEW] marker is left in place — concrete asks for Jackie / domain experts instead of a vague "send us material."

11.1.1 Financial modeling & valuation Forecasting · scenarios · valuation

Cluster scope. Decision-ready financial models and planning frameworks that translate intent into defensible forecasts, quantified risk scenarios, and valuation-backed capital decisions. Three categories below — staff to the right one.

Forecasting & Planning

Definition. Building integrated financial models (3-statement, driver-based, or operating models) that connect revenue drivers, cost structures, and cash flow dynamics — for planning, fundraising, or board decisions.

Not to be confused with. Budgeting / FP&A reporting (variance work, not model construction); scenario & sensitivity analysis (testing existing models); dashboard / KPI reporting (visualization on top, not the underlying model).

CV signals · positive. 3-statement build experience; driver-based forecasting; advanced Excel (INDIRECT, OFFSET, INDEX/MATCH, dynamic arrays); Anaplan / Adaptive / Cube / Pigment / Vena; FP&A or strategic finance roles; named deliverables ("Series B model," "5-year forecast," "operating plan").

CV signals · false positive. "Financial modeling" listed without a deliverable (often classroom exposure); "FP&A" alone (could be reporting only); junior IB analysts who built valuation models, not operating ones.

Adjacent / transferable from. Junior IB / MBB consulting (if they owned a build); corporate strategy with operating-plan exposure; founders who built their own model.

Levels. A2 supports; A3+ owns; A4 / M1 leads complex builds.

Scenario & Sensitivity Analysis

Definition. Structured scenario planning that quantifies upside, downside, and break-even outcomes on top of an existing model, to support decisions under uncertainty.

Not to be confused with. Forecasting & planning (the underlying model itself); operational risk management (different domain); Monte Carlo / quantitative simulation (a higher bar — flag if the request implies it).

CV signals · positive. Built sensitivity tables / data tables in Excel; scenario manager or named scenarios; tornado charts; break-even analysis; scenarios on revenue / margin / churn / unit economics with explicit assumption shifts.

CV signals · false positive. "Scenario planning" in a strategy-consulting context sometimes means qualitative futures planning, not financial sensitivity.

Adjacent / transferable from. Corporate development (M&A model sensitivities); equity research (bear / base / bull case); VC / PE associates (LBO sensitivities, multiple-on-money cases).

Levels. A3+ owns; A2 with supervision.

Company & Asset Valuation

Definition. Defensible valuation analysis using DCF, comparable companies / precedent transactions, LBO, or asset-based methods — for transactions, fundraising, or internal planning.

Not to be confused with. Real estate appraisal (different methodology); insurance / actuarial valuation (reserving and pricing, not corporate); forecasting & planning (forecast is an input to a DCF, but operating planning is a separate skill).

CV signals · positive. DCF / WACC build / terminal value; comparable companies; precedent transactions; LBO; IB analyst (M&A or coverage); PE associate; VC investor; corp dev; CFA charter or progress — weight against deliverable evidence. [NEEDS_EXPERT_REVIEW: how heavily to weight CFA progress vs. real deliverables]

CV signals · false positive. "Valuation" listed without a deliverable (often a touched comps page, not a built valuation); equity research juniors with public-company exposure but limited private-company nuance.

Adjacent / transferable from. Equity research (rigor transfers, private-company nuance may not); restructuring / TAS at Big 4; founders who raised institutional capital.

Levels. A3+ owns; A4 / M1 for complex / multi-method valuations.

11.1.2 Market research & intelligence Sizing · competitive · customer research

Cluster scope. Actionable market research and competitive intelligence to pressure-test strategy, de-risk new markets, and give leaders confidence in every move. "Market research" is one of the most overloaded terms on a CV — before staffing, force the requester to specify which of the three categories below applies. The skill profiles diverge meaningfully.

Market Sizing & Opportunity Assessment

Definition. Quantifying addressable markets (TAM / SAM / SOM), validating segment attractiveness, and prioritizing opportunities for entry, expansion, or investment. Output is a defensible number built from primary inputs and triangulated — not a desk-research summary.

Not to be confused with. Competitive analysis (sister category — that's about rivals); customer research (the buyer view, not the aggregate); forecasting & planning (a company's revenue forecast, not the market upper bound); industry overview / desk research (analyst-report copy-paste, not a built number).

CV signals · positive. TAM / SAM / SOM with shown methodology (top-down vs. bottom-up, ideally both with triangulation); MBB (BCG, Bain, McKinsey) on commercial DD or growth strategy; tier-2 strategy (L.E.K., Roland Berger, OC&C); industry analyst firms (Gartner, Forrester, IDC, Frost & Sullivan, Euromonitor, Mintel); tools: Statista, IBISWorld, Crunchbase, PitchBook, CB Insights, Capital IQ; methodology references: Fermi estimation, comparable-market analogy, bottoms-up unit-economics build.

CV signals · false positive. "Market research" as a one-line skill (often means "I read industry reports"); sell-side equity research (strong on listed-market sizing, less reps on private/emerging markets); IB pitchbook prep (single comps page from a broker report).

Adjacent / transferable from. Commercial due diligence at consulting (closest cousin); equity research / VC associates (industry sizing chops, confirm private-market exposure); product managers who built TAM decks for fundraising; industry analysts (strong methodology, sometimes light on commercial framing).

Levels. A2 supports under guidance; A3+ owns a build; A4 / M1 leads cross-segment or multi-geo sizing.

Competitive Analysis & Benchmarking

Definition. Systematic study of competitors — their offerings, positioning, pricing, GTM, performance, and strategy — to inform a client's competitive moves. Output: a defensible "where we stand and what to do about it" picture, not a list of rivals.

Not to be confused with. Market sizing (the size of the prize, not who's competing); customer research (the buyer view); industry overview (broader, less actionable); IB competitor pages (often pitchbook fodder copy-pasted from broker reports).

CV signals · positive. Frameworks named: Porter's Five Forces, strategic group mapping, value-curve / Blue Ocean canvas, Wardley mapping; concepts: battlecards, win / loss analysis, deal-loss program, competitor teardowns, feature parity matrix, pricing benchmark; roles: competitive intel at B2B SaaS, corporate strategy, MBB commercial DD, Klue / Crayon (CI platforms), product marketing; industry analyst work at Gartner (Magic Quadrant) or Forrester (Wave).

CV signals · false positive. "Competitor research" used loosely (could be "I checked their LinkedIn"); marketing roles that did "competitive monitoring" (surveillance is not analysis).

Adjacent / transferable from. Product marketing managers (positioning, battlecards); strategy consulting commercial DD analysts (closest cousin); sales enablement (battlecards, lighter on synthesis); industry analysts at Gartner / Forrester (strong methodology).

Levels. A2 supports; A3+ owns a competitive teardown; A4 / M1 for executive-facing playbooks.

Customer Research & Segmentation

Definition. Understanding buyer needs, pain points, decision drivers, and behaviors to inform product, marketing, or strategy decisions. Both qualitative (interviews, focus groups, ethnography) and quantitative (surveys, conjoint, max-diff, Van Westendorp) methods. Segmentation: clustering customers into actionable groups.

Not to be confused with. Market sizing (how big, not who); competitive analysis (rivals, not buyers); customer success or service ops (managing post-sale, not researching); A/B testing or product analytics (behavioral data on existing users, narrower scope); UX research (usability-focused on a product, not commercial / segmentation).

CV signals · positive. Methodologies: Jobs-to-be-Done (JTBD), customer interviews with documented protocol, conjoint analysis, max-diff, Van Westendorp pricing studies, Kano model, NPS programs, persona development; tools: Qualtrics, SurveyMonkey, UserTesting, Dovetail, Sawtooth (conjoint); roles: market research analyst (corporate or agency), consumer insights at FMCG / CPG, customer / commercial strategy at MBB, voice-of-customer programs.

CV signals · false positive. "Customer research" without methodology (could mean "interviewed 3 people" — look for sample sizes and methods); "user research" in a product context (real research, but usability-focused); marketing analysts who only ran reports on existing data vs. those who designed and conducted primary research.

Adjacent / transferable from. UX researchers (strong qualitative, scaffolding on commercial framing); marketing analytics (strong quantitative, less primary-research design); consumer insights at FMCG / CPG (deep methodology training); strategy consultants on customer / growth projects.

Levels. A2 supports (interview synthesis, survey support); A3+ owns research design and analysis; A4 / M1 leads end-to-end primary research programs.

11.1.3 Pitch decks & proposals Investor · board · RFP

Cluster scope. High-stakes pitch decks and proposals communicating strategy, value, and credibility to investors, executives, and buyers. Three categories with very different audiences and conventions — the investor-deck specialist often makes a poor RFP author and vice versa.

Investor & Fundraising Decks

Definition. Pitch decks for investors (VC, PE, angel, strategic) for fundraising rounds. Story arc + market + traction + financials + team, narrative-led, typically 10–15 slides. Output is judged on whether it gets meetings and term sheets.

Not to be confused with. Sales decks (different audience and motion); board presentations (sister category — internal, decision-oriented); product decks (feature-led, not story-led); IR materials at public companies (post-IPO communications, not fundraising).

CV signals · positive. Founder / CEO experience having raised institutional capital; ex-VC analyst (saw 100s of decks); strategy consultants on fundraising-support engagements; "raised $Xm Series A/B/C," "supported pitch for [investor]"; frameworks: Sequoia template, YC pitch guide, references to Suster / Rachleff.

CV signals · false positive. "Pitch deck" listed generically (could be sales pitchbook); "investor relations" (usually public-company IR, not private fundraising); junior IB pitchbook prep (single-page comps, not narrative).

Adjacent / transferable from. Investment bankers (deck rigor, but selling vs. raising); product marketing managers (positioning chops); content / brand strategy (story-craft).

Levels. A2 supports build; A3+ owns drafts; A4 / M1 leads investor-narrative work.

Board & Executive Presentations

Definition. High-stakes briefings for boards, executive teams, or steering committees to drive alignment and decisions. Strategy refreshes, performance reviews, capital requests, M&A reviews, transformation status. Decision-oriented, not narrative-led.

Not to be confused with. Investor decks (sister — external, fundraising); sales decks (different audience); status reports (text-heavy, less decision-forcing); all-hands / town halls (internal communication, not decision-making).

CV signals · positive. Corporate strategy at large enterprises; Chief of Staff or VP Strategy roles; ex-MBB consultants (board-deck reps are core); "board pack," "QBR," "ExCo presentation," "operating review," "steering committee deck"; comfort with the Pyramid Principle (Minto).

CV signals · false positive. "Presentations" listed broadly (could be training decks, conference talks, status updates); "made decks for the CEO" (rigor varies — push for specific board / ExCo experience).

Adjacent / transferable from. Management consultants (closest cousin); strategic finance roles (financial review pages); corp dev (M&A board-update muscle); chief of staff at growth-stage cos.

Levels. A3+ owns sections; A4 / M1 leads full decks; lead-level for executive polish.

Request for Proposal & Proposal Development

Definition. Structured proposals responding to formal RFPs (Request for Proposal). Win themes, technical / capability sections, pricing, compliance matrix, references. Procurement-grade rigor — every requirement has to be addressed.

Not to be confused with. Investor decks (fundraising); board presentations (internal); general business cases or ROI memos (internal decisions, not bid documents); sales proposals (informal, no procurement structure).

CV signals · positive. Capture management or proposal management roles (Lockheed, Booz Allen, Deloitte FedSec, BAE, General Dynamics, RTX); APMP certification; Shipley Method references; "won $Xm contract," "captured [agency] bid"; technical writers with bid experience.

CV signals · false positive. "Proposals" listed broadly (could be sales proposals, less rigorous); "wrote proposals" without contract value or named agency.

Adjacent / transferable from. Technical writers at federal contractors; sales engineers on regulated-industry deals; government-contracting backgrounds; Big 4 federal advisory.

Levels. A3+ owns sections; A4 / M1 leads bid coordination; lead-level for $5m+ pursuits.

11.1.4 Data dashboards & analytics Financial · KPI / BI · sales analytics

Cluster scope. Clear, reliable dashboards and KPI reporting on reconciled data so leadership has decision-ready performance visibility. Same tooling family across the three categories, but different domain expertise.

Financial Dashboard Development

Definition. Dashboards presenting financial data (P&L, cash flow, balance-sheet metrics, AR / AP, capex) for finance and exec leadership. Connects to the accounting / ERP stack and respects the close calendar.

Not to be confused with. Financial models (Excel-based, scenario-driven); FP&A reporting (often slide-based, not dashboards); KPI / BI dashboards (broader, may include non-financial); investor dashboards at fund admins (niche, different stack).

CV signals · positive. FP&A roles with explicit dashboard build experience; Tableau / Power BI / Looker / Sigma certifications; integrations with NetSuite, QuickBooks, Sage Intacct, SAP, Oracle, Workday Adaptive; "built monthly close dashboard," "automated CFO scorecard," "P&L by entity dashboard."

CV signals · false positive. "Excel dashboards" (could be one-off pivot tables); "financial reporting" (often static slide reports); BI tool listed without a financial-system integration named.

Adjacent / transferable from. FP&A analysts with BI fluency; data analysts at finance-heavy firms; corporate accounting with BI exposure; Big 4 finance transformation.

Levels. A2 supports; A3+ owns build; A4 / M1 for cross-system integrations.

KPI Reporting & Business Intelligence

Definition. Cross-functional KPI dashboards and the BI infrastructure underneath. The hard part is often defining the right metrics across functions, not the dashboard build itself.

Not to be confused with. Financial dashboards (finance-specific); sales performance analytics (sales-specific); product analytics (user behavior on a software product — Mixpanel, Amplitude); data engineering (pipelines, not visualization).

CV signals · positive. BI / data / business analyst roles; Tableau, Power BI, Looker, Mode, Sigma, Hex, Metabase; methodology: KPI design, "metrics tree," "OKR program rollout"; real SQL fluency; cross-functional projects ("harmonized metrics across sales / marketing / CS").

CV signals · false positive. "KPI tracking" without specific tools; "Tableau" listed but no published deliverable; "business intelligence" used vaguely.

Adjacent / transferable from. Data analysts (closest cousin); revenue analytics at SaaS; corporate strategy on metrics-tree projects; consulting on performance management.

Levels. A2 supports; A3+ owns dashboard build; A4 / M1 leads cross-functional metric design.

Sales Performance Analytics

Definition. Dashboards and analytics on sales / pipeline data: funnel metrics, rep performance, territory analytics, forecast accuracy, deal cycle, win rates.

Not to be confused with. KPI / BI reporting (broader); RevOps (process and data, see RevOps cluster); marketing analytics (top-of-funnel, attribution lens); financial dashboards (booked vs. billed vs. revenue-recognized treatment is different).

CV signals · positive. Sales Ops / RevOps analyst; Salesforce reports / dashboards (admin or CRT level); HubSpot, Outreach, Salesloft, Gong, Clari, BoostUp, InsightSquared; "pipeline analytics dashboard," "forecast accuracy program," "territory analysis," "rep performance scorecard"; MEDDPICC-aware metric design.

CV signals · false positive. Generic "sales analyst" with no system-specific evidence; "Salesforce" listed without admin / CRT depth.

Adjacent / transferable from. Sales ops / RevOps generalists; marketing analytics analysts; financial analysts on revenue side; Big 4 SaaS GTM analytics.

Levels. A2 supports; A3+ owns; A4 / M1 leads RevOps-adjacent analytics programs.

11.1.5 Business process optimization Workflow design · BPI · AI / automation

Cluster scope. High-efficiency process and automation design that removes friction, reduces manual overhead, and makes execution consistent. The three categories require different muscles — documentation / architecture, analytical KPI-driven improvement, and hands-on tooling. Don't assume cross-applicability.

Workflow Design & Process Mapping

Definition. Documenting and (re)designing end-to-end workflows. Process maps (current and future state), swimlanes, RACI, SOP creation. Output is a clear shared understanding of how work flows.

Not to be confused with. Business process improvement (sister — making processes better); change management (people-side adoption); project management (delivery); system / software design (IT architecture).

CV signals · positive. Lean / Six Sigma training (Yellow / Green / Black Belt); BPMN notation; SIPOC diagrams; value-stream maps; tools: Visio, Lucidchart, Miro, Whimsical, Bizagi; "as-is / to-be process maps," "SOP library," "RACI matrix for [function]"; ops or ops-consulting roles with named process work.

CV signals · false positive. "Process improvement" as a vague claim with no methodology; "documentation" experience that's product-doc rather than process-doc.

Adjacent / transferable from. Operations managers with documentation reps; consulting at ops-focused firms (West Monroe, Bain Operations); Lean practitioners from manufacturing; quality / compliance roles in regulated industries.

Levels. A2 supports SOP drafting; A3+ owns process maps; A4 / M1 leads cross-functional redesign.

Business Process Improvement

Definition. Identifying inefficiencies in existing processes and implementing improvements. More analytical than design — KPI-driven, often Lean / Six Sigma flavor. Output is a measurable reduction in cycle time, error rate, or cost.

Not to be confused with. Workflow design (sister — designing the process); change management (adoption); operations consulting (broader); continuous improvement (culture-level practice; BPI is the project work inside it).

CV signals · positive. Lean Six Sigma certifications (Green / Black / Master Black Belt); DMAIC methodology; specific savings claims with methodology ("reduced cycle time by 40% via DMAIC," "eliminated $Xm of waste"); manufacturing or operations backgrounds; Big 4 Op Ex practices.

CV signals · false positive. "Operational excellence" as a vague claim; "improved efficiency" without quantified impact; "Lean" mentioned without certification.

Adjacent / transferable from. Operations managers with measurable improvement track records; manufacturing engineers; Big 4 Op Ex / Bain Operations Excellence; continuous-improvement leads at scaled organizations.

Levels. A3+ owns assessments; A4 / M1 leads BPI programs; lead-level for cross-functional transformation.

AI & Automation Integration

Definition. Integrating AI tools and workflow automation (RPA, low-code) into existing processes. Practical — not building new AI models or custom software, but applying available tooling to remove manual work.

Not to be confused with. AI / ML engineering (building models); data science (analysis); custom software development (building applications); workflow design (sister — designing the process).

CV signals · positive. Tools: Zapier, Make (Integromat), n8n, Workato, Tray.io; RPA: UiPath, Automation Anywhere, Blue Prism, Power Automate; low-code: Airtable, Bubble, Retool, Glide, Webflow; LLM: OpenAI / Anthropic API, Claude API, agent frameworks; specific evidence: "automated [process] saving X hours/week," "built RPA bot for [task]."

CV signals · false positive. "Automation" listed broadly (could be batch scripts or scheduled jobs); "AI experience" without named tool, integration, or output; "ChatGPT user" listed as a skill.

Adjacent / transferable from. RevOps engineers (automation reps inside the GTM stack); operations managers with named automation projects; tech-fluent generalists (often founders / first ops hires); Gratia's E1 engineering track (core territory).

Levels. A2 supports simple Zapier flows; A3+ owns multi-tool integrations; E1 track for complex builds; A4 / M1 for cross-system automation programs.

11.1.6 Project management Planning · risk & controls · reporting

Cluster scope. End-to-end project management and controls to ship critical initiatives on time, within scope, and tied to measurable business value. "PM" on a CV is one of the broadest claims out there — force the request to specify what kind of project (size, complexity, regulated, budget responsibility) before staffing.

Project Planning & Setup

Definition. Scoping, structuring, and launching projects. Charters, plans, governance design, kickoff, stakeholder alignment, RACI. Output is a project set up to succeed, not just one that exists.

Not to be confused with. Project execution / delivery (ongoing day-to-day, what most "PMs" do); portfolio management (multiple unrelated projects); program management (multiple related projects to a common outcome); product management (defining what to build).

CV signals · positive. Certifications: PMP, PRINCE2 (Practitioner), CAPM, Scrum Master; specific evidence: "led project kickoff for $Xm program," "developed project charter for [initiative]," "stood up PMO"; tools: MS Project, Smartsheet, Asana, Monday, Jira, ClickUp; comfort with charters, scope statements, WBS.

CV signals · false positive. "Managed projects" as a generic claim — everyone does. Look for charters and governance evidence. Scrum certifications without project-leadership reps. "Coordinated" used where "led" is needed.

Adjacent / transferable from. Management consultants (kickoff / structuring muscle); engineering or tech project managers with named programs; operations / program managers at growth-stage cos; construction or infrastructure PMs.

Levels. A2 supports planning artifacts; A3+ owns smaller projects; A4 / M1 leads complex multi-stakeholder programs.

Risk Management & Project Controls

Definition. Structured identification, assessment, and mitigation of project risks, plus the controls keeping projects on track (cost, schedule, scope, quality). Earned Value Management, RAID logs, change-control governance.

Not to be confused with. Enterprise risk management / ERM (broader, finance / compliance domain); financial risk (credit, market, liquidity); operational risk (process-level, steady-state ops); cybersecurity risk (specialized adjacent domain).

CV signals · positive. Risk registers, RAID logs, EVM (Earned Value Management); PMI-RMP certification; PRINCE2 risk module; specific evidence ("managed $Xm project budget," "led EVM reporting for [program]"); construction / infra / aerospace backgrounds; PMO analyst roles with controls responsibility.

CV signals · false positive. "Risk assessment" without methodology or named project; "project controls" used loosely to mean status reporting.

Adjacent / transferable from. PMO analysts at large enterprises; large-program PMs at construction / infra (Bechtel, Fluor, Jacobs); Big 4 risk advisory; aerospace / defense program managers.

Levels. A3+ owns risk registers; A4 / M1 leads program-level risk governance; lead-level for regulated / multi-billion programs.

Project Reporting & Performance Tracking

Definition. Reports and dashboards showing project status, performance against plan, and decision points for sponsors. Steering committee materials, executive dashboards, milestone tracking.

Not to be confused with. Financial dashboards (separate cluster); KPI / BI dashboards (broader); status updates (less rigorous); stand-ups / retros (agile rituals, not formal reporting).

CV signals · positive. PMO reporting experience; "executive program update," "steering committee report," "burndown / burn-up charts," "milestone tracker for [program]"; project dashboard tooling: Smartsheet dashboards, Power BI on Jira / MS Project, Asana portfolios.

CV signals · false positive. Generic "status reports" without dashboard or analytical rigor; "PMO" listed as a skill without specific reporting reps.

Adjacent / transferable from. PMO analysts (closest cousin); program managers with reporting cadence; consulting on transformation programs; strategic finance roles supporting large-initiative reviews.

Levels. A2 supports; A3+ owns project reporting cadence; A4 / M1 for portfolio-level reporting.

11.1.7 Growth & go-to-market strategy GTM · market entry · revenue growth

Cluster scope. Integrated go-to-market and expansion planning that aligns product, pricing, and channels — turning growth goals into repeatable playbooks. The three categories have very different success criteria: full GTM design, geographic / segment expansion, and squeeze-the-base revenue growth.

GTM Strategy & Planning

Definition. Comprehensive go-to-market plans aligning product, pricing, channel, sales motion, and marketing for a new product, segment, or launch. Output is a launch playbook, not a single-deck strategy.

Not to be confused with. Marketing strategy (subset — demand, brand, content); sales strategy (subset — segments, motion, comp); product strategy (what to build, not how to sell); revenue growth strategy (sister — squeeze the base, not new motion).

CV signals · positive. Head of Marketing / VP Marketing at growth-stage cos; Product Marketing Manager (PMM) roles with launch reps; MBB on growth strategy; "led GTM for [product launch]," "ICP definition," "channel strategy"; familiarity with PLG vs. SLG vs. hybrid motion debates.

CV signals · false positive. "Go-to-market" as a vague skill (everyone in marketing claims it); sales reps who attended GTM planning meetings vs. designed them.

Adjacent / transferable from. PMMs (closest cousin); sales leaders with strategy reps; growth-stage marketing leaders; MBB on growth strategy.

Levels. A3+ supports GTM workstreams; A4 / M1 owns components; lead-level for full GTM design.

Market Entry & Expansion

Definition. Entry strategies for new geographies, segments, or verticals. Feasibility assessment, in-target competitive landscape, partnership / channel decisions, regulatory / localization, beachhead selection.

Not to be confused with. Market sizing (research cluster — an input, not the entry strategy); competitive analysis (research cluster — an input); fundraising (capital, not strategy); GTM strategy (sister — how to sell vs. where).

CV signals · positive. International expansion with named geographies; "led entry into [country / region]"; MBB / tier-2 strategy on market-entry projects; multi-country regulatory / operational experience; familiarity with entry-mode trade-offs (greenfield vs. JV vs. acquisition vs. license vs. distributor).

CV signals · false positive. "Global experience" (could be working across multiple offices, not real expansion strategy); "international" team membership without named entry / launch reps.

Adjacent / transferable from. International business development (closest cousin if they led entry); corporate strategy at multinationals; cross-border M&A consulting; regional GMs at scaling cos.

Levels. A3+ supports research and analysis; A4 / M1 owns subset of strategy; lead-level for primary engagements.

Revenue Growth Strategy

Definition. Identifying and capturing revenue opportunities within an existing business: pricing optimization, cross-sell / upsell programs, win-rate improvement, churn / retention work, packaging redesign.

Not to be confused with. GTM strategy (sister — new products / new markets); RevOps (separate cluster — operational, not strategic); financial planning / forecasting (projecting, not designing); marketing campaigns (tactical demand gen).

CV signals · positive. "Led pricing optimization saving $Xm," "designed cross-sell program," "retention strategy reducing churn from Y% to Z%"; pricing-specialist consulting (Simon-Kucher, BCG pricing, McKinsey pricing); CS leadership with retention reps; growth-team roles at SaaS; understanding of NRR / GRR / DRR mechanics.

CV signals · false positive. "Drove revenue growth" (generic exec summary); sales reps who hit quota vs. designed the program that lifted everyone's quota.

Adjacent / transferable from. Pricing consultants (closest cousin); Customer Success leaders (retention / expansion); growth marketing leaders (top-of-funnel); strategic finance / CFO offices on revenue plans.

Levels. A3+ supports analysis; A4 / M1 owns workstreams; lead-level for end-to-end strategy framing.

11.1.8 RevOps & sales enablement Sales process · RevOps setup · enablement

Cluster scope. Integrated revenue architectures aligning stages, handoffs, reporting, and sales enablement to shorten cycles and improve conversion. "RevOps" is a young discipline and the title gets used loosely — the three categories split tightly.

Sales Process Optimization & CRM

Definition. Design and tuning of the sales process and CRM architecture. Stage definitions, opportunity management, lead routing, deal-desk hygiene, forecast methodology, automation. Output is a sales motion that's measurable and predictable.

Not to be confused with. Sales strategy (what to sell); sales enablement (sister — training and content); marketing automation (top-of-funnel: Marketo, Pardot); CS ops (post-sale).

CV signals · positive. Sales Ops / RevOps with named CRM rebuilds; Salesforce certs (Admin, Advanced Admin, Sales Cloud Consultant, CPQ); HubSpot certs; Outreach, Salesloft, Gong, Clari, BoostUp; "rebuilt deal stages," "MEDDIC / MEDDPICC implementation," "Sales Cloud build for [team size]," "lead routing rebuild."

CV signals · false positive. "Salesforce" listed as a skill (often user-level, not admin / architect); "worked with the sales team on process" (vague, no design ownership).

Adjacent / transferable from. RevOps engineers (more technical depth); sales ops managers; SaaS implementation consultants; Salesforce consulting firms (Slalom, Silverline, IBM Consulting).

Levels. A2 supports config; A3+ owns process design; A4 / M1 leads end-to-end RevOps projects.

Revenue Operations Setup

Definition. Standing up the connective tissue across Sales / Marketing / Customer Success — shared data layer, agreed metrics, lifecycle handoffs, attribution model, lead-to-revenue pipeline. The "RevOps" function itself.

Not to be confused with. Sales process / CRM (sister — sales-only scope); marketing operations (marketing-only); CS operations (CS-only); GTM strategy (separate cluster — strategic, not operational).

CV signals · positive. VP / Head of RevOps roles, especially first-RevOps-hire stories; "built RevOps function from zero," "designed GTM analytics platform," "lead-to-revenue dashboard," "harmonized metrics across sales / marketing / CS"; tooling integrations across SF / HS + marketing automation + CS tools (Gainsight, Catalyst, Vitally); lifecycle metric fluency (MQL → SQL → Opp → Closed-Won → NRR).

CV signals · false positive. "Revenue operations" listed loosely without scope; sales ops or marketing ops claimed as RevOps without cross-functional reps.

Adjacent / transferable from. GTM ops generalists at scale-up SaaS; RevOps consultancies (Iceberg, Winning by Design, Pavilion-adjacent); BizOps generalists at growth-stage cos.

Levels. A3+ supports buildout; A4 / M1 owns RevOps function design; lead-level for end-to-end programs.

Sales Enablement & Tools

Definition. Equipping sellers with messaging, materials, training, and supporting tools (LMS, content management, AI assistants) to win more deals with less effort.

Not to be confused with. Sales process (sister — system and process); product marketing (positioning at audience level); training (general — broader); HR onboarding (cross-company).

CV signals · positive. Sales Enablement Manager / Director roles; tools: Highspot, Seismic, Showpad, Lessonly, MindTickle, Brainshark, Gong (call coaching); "battlecards for [competitor / segment]," "playbook for [motion]," "rep onboarding program," "sales certification program"; PMM-adjacent with explicit enablement responsibilities.

CV signals · false positive. "Trained the team" (could be a single workshop, not a program); "sales coaching" without curriculum, certification, or measurable rep ramp impact.

Adjacent / transferable from. PMMs with enablement reps; sales trainers (closest cousin if they design programs); instructional designers in B2B context; L&D leads in revenue functions.

Levels. A3+ supports content; A4 / M1 owns enablement programs; lead-level for full enablement function.

11.1.9 Business strategy services Corporate · planning · business model

Cluster scope. Strategy options backed by research, scenarios, and quantified impact so leadership can set priorities and move with confidence. "Strategy consultant" is one of the most-claimed and least-rigorously-defined roles on a CV — force the request to specify which of the three categories applies.

Corporate & Growth Strategy

Definition. Where to play and how to win. Portfolio decisions, M&A strategy, growth bets, capital allocation, vertical / horizontal expansion, divestiture rationale. The classic "strategy" work that lands in front of a CEO or board.

Not to be confused with. Growth marketing / growth tactics (campaigns and programs); GTM strategy (separate cluster — executing within a defined market); FP&A / financial planning; strategic planning (sister — translating strategy into a plan).

CV signals · positive. MBB strategy projects (Bain, BCG, McKinsey) — not just tier-2 implementation work; tier-2 strategy (L.E.K., Roland Berger, OC&C, Oliver Wyman strategy); corporate strategy at large enterprises (VP / SVP-level); ex-IB in strategy roles; "led 3-year strategy refresh," "M&A strategy and target ID," "portfolio review."

CV signals · false positive. "Strategy consultant" used loosely (everyone in consulting calls themselves a strategist); operations / implementation projects claimed as strategy work.

Adjacent / transferable from. Corp dev (M&A execution side, lighter on portfolio strategy); Chief of Staff / strategic-projects roles; PE operating partners (value-creation strategy); equity research in strategic roles post-research.

Levels. A3+ supports analysis; A4 / M1 owns workstreams; lead-level for full strategy engagements.

Strategic Planning & Roadmapping

Definition. Translating strategy into a sequenced, milestone-based plan over multiple years. OKRs, multi-year roadmaps, capacity planning, dependency mapping, resourcing decisions.

Not to be confused with. Project planning (separate cluster — project-specific); product roadmapping (product-specific); annual budgeting (financial-specific); corporate strategy (sister — the strategy itself, not the multi-year execution layer).

CV signals · positive. "Led 5-year strategic plan," "annual operating plan," "OKR program rollout for [function / company]"; roles: VP Strategy, Chief of Staff, head-of-strategy-execution; frameworks: Hoshin Kanri, V2MOM, OKR (Doerr / Grove tradition), Balanced Scorecard; cross-functional rollout reps (designed AND drove adoption).

CV signals · false positive. "Strategic planning" used to describe annual budgeting or project planning; "OKRs" listed as a skill without rollout / adoption reps.

Adjacent / transferable from. Management consulting on operating-plan engagements; corp dev (close on integration roadmapping); Chief of Staff functions; transformation program leads at large enterprises.

Levels. A3+ supports; A4 / M1 owns roadmap design; lead-level for cross-functional alignment.

Business Model Design

Definition. Defining or redefining how a business creates, delivers, and captures value. New business unit launches, monetization redesign, platform / marketplace strategy, ecosystem positioning, pricing-model overhaul.

Not to be confused with. Financial modeling (the math); GTM strategy (how to sell); product strategy (what to build); revenue growth strategy (squeeze-the-base, not structural change).

CV signals · positive. "Designed new business unit for [company]," "monetization strategy for [product]," "platform / marketplace design," "pricing-model overhaul"; founder / first-employee experience at companies that did meaningful business-model pivots; Business Model Canvas / Lean Canvas familiarity (with reps applying); platform / marketplace consulting (Applico, Roland Berger digital, BCG Digital Ventures).

CV signals · false positive. "Business model" listed without specific design work or named pivot; "innovation" used as a stand-in (often broad and vague).

Adjacent / transferable from. Founders who designed monetization ground-up; corporate venture builders / venture studios; platform / marketplace strategists; ecosystem strategy consultants.

Levels. A3+ supports analysis and option-generation; A4 / M1 owns components; lead-level for full business-model design engagements.

11.2.1 Private credit Direct lending · mezzanine · distressed

Definition. Non-bank lending to companies, typically by investment funds. Sub-segments: direct lending (senior secured loans to mid-market), mezzanine, distressed / special situations, venture debt. Borrowers are often PE-owned or pre-IPO. The fund holds the loan rather than syndicating it through a public market.

Not to be confused with. M&A advisory / general IB (advises on equity transactions, not lending — the most common false positive); public credit / fixed income trading (markets-driven on listed paper); commercial banking (different regulatory framework, different risk tolerance); actuarial / insurance underwriting (same word, different domain).

CV signals · positive. Firms: Apollo, Ares, Golub, Blackstone Credit (formerly GSO), KKR Credit, Owl Rock / Blue Owl, Sixth Street, Oaktree, Antares, Monroe, Audax, BlackRock Private Credit, Fortress. Functions: origination, credit underwriting, portfolio monitoring, restructuring / workouts. Concepts in CV: unitranche, first lien / second lien, PIK toggle, revolver, DDTL, ABL, covenant-lite, EBITDA add-backs, OID. Loan servicing / fund admin for direct lending vehicles (operations angle).

CV signals · false positive. "Worked on a credit deal at Goldman" — was the candidate selling to credit funds (advisory) or buying for a credit fund (investing)? Different jobs. M&A bankers at firms with credit affiliates sometimes claim "credit experience" without underwriting reps.

Adjacent / transferable from. Leveraged finance / LevFin at IB (same documents, transactional vs. holding mindset); restructuring / TAS at Big 4 (strong on distressed, lighter on origination); corporate or middle-market banking (close, different risk tolerance and structures); PE deal-team analysts (debt understood from the equity-sponsor seat).

Common ask wedge. "Private credit experience" is usually one of three things — clarify before searching: (1) credit underwriting rigor, (2) document fluency (credit agreements, term sheets, amendments), (3) operations / middle-office for a credit fund. The ideal candidate differs significantly across the three. [NEEDS_EXPERT_REVIEW: validate firm list, sub-segment definitions, and the three-way ask-wedge framing]

Levels. A3+ for credit analysis support; A4 / M1 for memo or covenant ownership; lead-level for client-facing engagement on origination or portfolio work.

11.2.2 Insurance · regulatory & operations NAIC · stat accounting · Solvency II

Definition. The insurance sector, with emphasis on regulatory compliance, statutory accounting and reporting (NAIC in the US), and operations supporting insurance companies. Sub-segments: P&C, Life, Health, Reinsurance. Scoped here to the regulatory / operations / advisory angle — actuarial pricing and reserving is a separate specialism.

Not to be confused with. Actuarial science / pricing / reserving (mortality and claims modeling — usually requires FSA / FCAS / ACAS); insurance brokerage / sales (selling policies); insurtech (software for the industry); healthcare consulting (different domain — provider / payer ops); bank regulatory consulting (similar muscle, different rulebook).

CV signals · positive. Firms: AIG, MetLife, Prudential, Allianz, Zurich, Munich Re, Swiss Re, Hartford, Travelers, Chubb, Aon, Marsh, WTW, Big 4 insurance practices. Concepts: NAIC reporting, stat accounting (STAT vs. GAAP), Schedule D, RBC, ORSA, Solvency II, IFRS 17, ALM. Deliverables: annual / quarterly stat filings, RBC calculations, loss ratio analysis, stat-to-GAAP reconciliation.

CV signals · false positive. "Insurance" in the CV because the candidate had an insurance client at a strategy consulting firm but did generic strategy work. "Risk management" at non-insurance firms (different risk).

Adjacent / transferable from. Big 4 audit (insurance practice); actuarial analysts without full credentials; bank regulatory consulting (Basel / CCAR); corporate accounting at an insurer.

Common ask wedge. Three buckets — ask which: (1) regulatory project support (NAIC / state filings); (2) investment accounting for insurers (where State Street PAM-type system experience overlaps); (3) operational transformation (policy admin, claims, billing). The Venn of analysts who can credibly do all three is small. [NEEDS_EXPERT_REVIEW: insurance practitioner to validate concept list, ask-wedge, and adjacent-skill weighting]

Levels. A3+ for stat reporting support; A4 / M1 for ownership of regulatory project workstreams.

11.2.3 Investment banking · M&A advisory Sell-side / buy-side · IPOs · valuation

Definition. Sell-side and buy-side advisory on equity transactions: M&A, IPOs, strategic alternatives, fairness opinions, capital markets transactions. The bank advises and supports execution; it does not take principal risk on the transaction. Included here both as a real client request and as the canonical disambiguator for private-credit and other finance asks.

Not to be confused with. Private credit / direct lending (see 11.2.1 — the most common false positive); equity research (same firms, no transactions); sales & trading (markets, not advisory); corporate development (in-house M&A, one client only); private equity (buys companies, uses IB); venture capital (early-stage investing).

CV signals · positive. Bulge bracket: GS, MS, JPM, BofA, Citi, Barclays, CS / UBS, DB. Elite boutiques: Evercore, Lazard, Centerview, Moelis, HL, Jefferies, PJT, Guggenheim. Middle market: Harris Williams, William Blair, Lincoln, Piper Sandler, Stifel, Raymond James. Concepts: pitched / ran diligence / managed data room / drafted CIM / executed deal / negotiated SPA / fairness opinion. Deliverables: pitch decks, IM, valuation models (DCF / comps / precedents / LBO), management presentations, board materials.

CV signals · false positive. "Investment banking" used loosely (could mean any function at a bank); SA / internship-only experience (pitchbook prep, not execution); "worked on M&A" without a named transaction.

Adjacent / transferable from. Big 4 transaction advisory (strong on diligence, lighter on execution); corp dev (buy-side perspective, fewer reps); PE associates (combine IB execution and investing); MBB on commercial-due-diligence projects (adjacent to IB diligence).

Common pitfall. Recommending an IB analyst for a private-credit role is the canonical false positive. Same modeling rigor, fundamentally different job: IB is transactional / deal-by-deal; private credit is portfolio / hold-and-monitor. Likewise, IB analysts asked to do operating-plan / forecasting work often struggle — they have built valuation models, not operating ones. Confirm what the client actually needs.

Levels. A3+ for support on IB-flavored work; A4 / M1 for ownership; lead-level for client-facing on deal-flavored projects.
